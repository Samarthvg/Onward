    How it works

    What every posting's details mean. Everything here is worked out on this computer, from the postings' own words.

    Match scores

    A score is how much of what a posting asks for is on your resume.

    Skills
    The skills and tools in a posting's requirements are checked word for word against your resume. Required ones count fully and nice-to-have ones half. Rare skills (Rust, CUDA) count more than ones nearly every posting names (Git). Sentences with no concrete skill, like "strong communication", aren't scored, since a resume can't settle them.
    Confidence
    Every score is mixed with a few average skills at half credit, so matching 3 of 3 can't look as sure as 14 of 21. A posting that names only a few skills stays near the middle, and even a perfect match stays under 100%. So that such a posting isn't lost, a match filter or the apply queue's minimum never leaves it out when you have the skills and years it requires.
    Experience
    Your years come from the dates on your resume, internships counting half. A posting asking for more lowers the score; one that doesn't say lowers it a little.
    Degree
    Shown for your information, not part of the score.

    Have a skill it missed, or different years? Fix it under Review skills (next to your resume); scores update at once.

    Posting dates

    Posted
    The date the company's own job board gives, read straight from it. "Check it yourself" opens that record.
    Date only
    Some boards (Workday) give the day but not the time. When Onward first saw a posting on that same day, that's the latest it could have gone up.
    Last updated
    A few boards give only when a posting was last edited, so it may be older than it looks. Those postings say so.
    First saw it
    When Onward's own search first found the posting: its own record, whatever the board says.
    Up 5 weeks, Reposted
    A role up for weeks has often been filled already. A repost is a role taken down and put up again with a new date; it counts as new, and its badge says when it first went up.

    Apply queue

    What goes in
    New postings from the last 7 days at or above your minimum match (Queue settings), that pass the queue's sponsorship and location filters. One that names too few skills to judge goes in when you have the ones it requires.
    Resume
    Your master resume when it already has what the posting asks for, or when tailoring could only reorder it. Otherwise a tailored copy: only skills your resume backs, no new numbers, still one page. Approve it, or choose the master instead.
    Answers
    Drafts for the form's own questions, where the site publishes them ahead. The extension fills them marked "AI answer, check it". Sponsorship, demographic, salary and legal questions are never drafted.
    Daily runs
    Tailoring and drafting each use one of the day's model runs (Queue settings); when they run out, the rest wait for tomorrow.
    The posting's words
    "No sponsorship" and "no OPT" come from the posting itself, quoted under its details. They outrank the company's history.
    H-1B history
    From USCIS's H-1B Employer Data Hub: how many approvals the company had. None found can mean the company files under another legal name (Meta Platforms, not Facebook).

      Apply kit

      Your details for application forms. Saved only in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.

      About me, for drafted answers

      Written answers ("Why this company?") can only mention things you tell them. Write a few lines in your own words: what you're into, why certain areas excite you, products you use, anything true you'd say in an interview. Drafts use this and never invent beyond it.

      Onward Autofill extension

      If you've installed the extension (see extension/README.md), it replaces the bookmark below: it also attaches your resume, picks dropdown answers and remembers answers you type. Open its options and press Import from dashboard while this page is open, or copy your details here and paste them there.

      One-click autofill on application forms

      This works as a special bookmark. Instead of opening a page, clicking it fills in the form you're looking at with the details above. It never submits anything.

      1. Show your bookmarks toolbar: press Ctrl+Shift+B (Firefox, Chrome and Edge).
      2. Drag this green button onto the toolbar:
        Fill application
        Dragging doesn't work? Click Copy bookmark, right-click the bookmarks toolbar, choose Add Bookmark…, name it "Fill application", paste into the URL field, and save.
      3. On a job's application form (the Apply ↗ button opens one), click the bookmark. Then attach your resume, answer the remaining questions, review, and submit yourself.

      Works on Greenhouse and Ashby forms and on Workday. Workday asks you to sign in first, and each company's Workday site needs its own account; make it yourself, then click the bookmark on each step (My Information, My Experience). It never clicks Next or answers sponsorship, work authorization, or demographic questions.

      Try it here first

      Changed your details? Make the bookmark again; it carries the details from when it was made.
