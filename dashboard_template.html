      How it works

      What every posting's details mean. Everything here is worked out on this computer, from the postings' own words.

      Match scores

      A score is how much of what a posting asks for is on your resume.

      Skills
      The skills and tools in a posting's requirements are checked word for word against your resume. Required ones count fully and nice-to-have ones half. Rare skills (Rust, CUDA) count more than ones nearly every posting names (Git). Sentences with no concrete skill, like "strong communication", aren't scored, since a resume can't settle them.
      Confidence
      Every score is mixed with a few average skills at half credit, so matching 3 of 3 can't look as sure as 14 of 21. A posting that names only a few skills stays near the middle, and even a perfect match stays under 100%. So that such a posting isn't lost, a match filter or the apply queue's minimum never leaves it out when you have the skills and years it requires. One that requires no skill at all, only a nice-to-have or two, is marked "Unclear fit": it stays in All jobs but isn't picked or queued, since nothing says you have what the job needs.
      Experience
      Your years come from the dates on your resume, internships counting half. A posting asking for more lowers the score; one that doesn't say lowers it a little.
      Degree
      Shown for your information, not part of the score.

      Have a skill it missed, or different years? Fix it under Review skills (next to your resume); scores update at once.

      Posting dates

      Posted
      The date the company's own job board gives, read straight from it. "Check it yourself" opens that record.
      Date only
      Some boards (Workday) give the day but not the time. When Onward first saw a posting on that same day, that's the latest it could have gone up.
      Last updated
      A few boards give only when a posting was last edited, so it may be older than it looks. Those postings say so.
      First saw it
      When Onward's own search first found the posting: its own record, whatever the board says.
      Up 5 weeks, Reposted
      A role up for weeks has often been filled already. A repost is a role taken down and put up again with a new date; it counts as new, and its badge says when it first went up.

      Today's picks

      The few new postings most worth applying to, at the top of the Apply queue: from the last 2 days (7 when that's too few), at 60% match or more, best first. Postings you've applied to or dismissed, that rule out sponsorship or OPT for your visa, or that ask for more years than you have are left out. One the queue has prepared shows its resume and answers; any other, Tailor and Apply. Choose how many a day beside the heading.

      Apply queue

      What goes in
      New postings from the last 7 days at or above your minimum match (Queue settings), that one of your roles wants and whose work authorization rule (Settings, Roles) doesn't rule out: with OPT, postings that say no sponsorship or no OPT stay out. One that names too few skills to judge goes in when you have the ones it requires. It holds your best matches up to a number you set (15 to start), with newer postings ahead: each day since a posting went up costs it 1.5 points of match in the queue's ranking, so a posting from today beats a slightly better one from last week, and a clearly better match still wins. A posting leaves once it's more than 7 days old. The others wait, and come in already prepared as you apply to or dismiss the ones before them, also ones that missed out on a crowded day.
      Resume
      Your master resume when it already has what the posting asks for, or when tailoring could only reorder it. Otherwise a tailored copy: only skills your resume backs, no new numbers, still one page. Approve it, or choose the master instead.
      Answers
      Drafts for the form's own questions, where the site publishes them ahead. The extension fills them marked "AI answer, check it". Sponsorship, demographic, salary and legal questions are never drafted.
      Daily runs
      Tailoring and drafting each use one of the day's model runs (Queue settings); when they run out, the rest wait for tomorrow.

      Visa badges

      The posting's words
      "No sponsorship" and "no OPT" come from the posting itself, quoted under its details. They outrank the company's history.
      H-1B history
      From USCIS's H-1B Employer Data Hub: how many H-1Bs the company had approved in the two newest fiscal years, refreshed every three months as USCIS adds a quarter. "New" are new H-1B jobs; the rest are extensions, transfers and amendments. None found can mean the company files under another legal name (Meta Platforms, not Facebook).

        Apply kit

        What the Onward Autofill extension puts into application forms. It's kept on this computer, by the app.

        Standard questions

        Voluntary self-identification

        Asked on most US applications and never used to decide on you. "Decline to answer" is always fine.

        Job site accounts

        Workday has you make an account on each company's own site. With a password here, the extension fills those sign-in and create-account pages with your email above and this password, then leaves the button to you. Use a password only for job sites, not the one for your email.

        About me, for drafted answers

        Written answers ("Why this company?") can only mention things you tell them. Write a few lines in your own words: what you're into, why certain areas excite you, products you use, anything true about you that a resume doesn't say. Drafts use only what's here and in your resume.

        Skills read from your resume

        Match scores compare these against the skills listed in each job. Click a skill to turn it off if it was misread. Add any the parser missed.
